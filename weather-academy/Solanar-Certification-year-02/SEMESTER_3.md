# Semester III · Year II Weeks 1–18

## Course equivalents

### ATM 231 — Dynamical Meteorology I (4 credit-equivalent hours)
Scale analysis; rotating-fluid dynamics; geostrophic and gradient wind; thermal wind; vorticity, divergence, vertical motion, baroclinicity and quasigeostrophic diagnosis.

### ATM 241 — Synoptic & Mesoscale Analysis I (4 credit-equivalent hours)
Fronts, cyclogenesis, jet dynamics, potential-vorticity thinking, mesoscale boundaries and integrated surface/upper-air diagnosis.

### MAT 251 — Ordinary Differential Equations for Atmospheric Science (3 credit-equivalent hours)
First- and second-order ODEs, systems, stability, numerical integration and atmospheric applications.

### ATM 251 — Radar, Satellite & Remote Sensing II (3 credit-equivalent hours)
Doppler radar, dual-polarization concepts, multispectral satellite analysis, derived products, sampling limits and quality control.

### ATM 261 — Numerical Weather Prediction I (2 credit-equivalent hours)
Model formulation, grids, discretization, initialization, parameterization, verification and forecast-system limitations.

### OPS 231 — Operational Forecast Laboratory I (2 credit-equivalent hours)
Daily analysis, forecast preparation, uncertainty, verification, briefing and decision-support practice.

## Weekly curriculum

### Year II Week 1 · Program Week 37 — Scale Analysis & Equations of Atmospheric Motion
- **Monday — Dynamics / Theory:** Derive the force terms in the horizontal momentum equations and use scale analysis to identify dominant balances at synoptic and mesoscale scales.
- **Tuesday — Quantitative Methods:** Review first-order ordinary differential equations, dimensional analysis, nondimensional numbers and characteristic time/length scales.
- **Wednesday — Analysis Laboratory:** Diagnose which forces matter most in contrasting synoptic, tropical and mesoscale flow regimes.
- **Thursday — Remote Sensing / NWP:** Compare observed winds with model pressure/geopotential fields and identify where simple balance assumptions fail.
- **Friday — Operational Forecast Shift:** Produce a 24-hour forecast discussion that explicitly states the assumed dynamical balance and where ageostrophic effects may matter.
- **Required forms:** Synoptic Diagnostic Worksheet, Operational Forecast Shift Log
- **Competencies:** dynamics, scale-analysis, forecast-reasoning

### Year II Week 2 · Program Week 38 — Geostrophic, Gradient & Cyclostrophic Wind
- **Monday — Dynamics / Theory:** Develop geostrophic, gradient and cyclostrophic balances and connect curvature and pressure-gradient forces to real flow.
- **Tuesday — Quantitative Methods:** Solve linear first-order ODEs and vector-component problems; calculate geostrophic wind from height/pressure gradients.
- **Wednesday — Analysis Laboratory:** Compare observed wind with geostrophic/gradient-wind estimates around highs, lows and strongly curved flow.
- **Thursday — Remote Sensing / NWP:** Use upper-air analyses and model fields to identify supergradient/subgradient and ageostrophic regions.
- **Friday — Operational Forecast Shift:** Brief expected wind evolution and identify locations where balanced-wind assumptions are least reliable.
- **Required forms:** Synoptic Diagnostic Worksheet, Professional Forecast Briefing
- **Competencies:** balanced-flow, wind-diagnosis, quantitative-analysis

### Year II Week 3 · Program Week 39 — Hydrostatic Balance, Thickness & Thermal Wind
- **Monday — Dynamics / Theory:** Connect hydrostatic balance, hypsometric thickness and thermal-wind relationships to horizontal temperature gradients and vertical shear.
- **Tuesday — Quantitative Methods:** Solve separable ODEs and hydrostatic integrations; calculate thickness and thermal-wind vectors.
- **Wednesday — Analysis Laboratory:** Analyze thickness, temperature advection and vertical shear on a multi-level synoptic case.
- **Thursday — Remote Sensing / NWP:** Compare sounding and model-derived thermal-wind structure with satellite cloud patterns.
- **Friday — Operational Forecast Shift:** Create a baroclinic-zone briefing with expected jet/front evolution and temperature-advection impacts.
- **Required forms:** Upper-Air & Thermodynamic Analysis, Synoptic Diagnostic Worksheet
- **Competencies:** hydrostatic, thermal-wind, baroclinicity

### Year II Week 4 · Program Week 40 — Vorticity, Divergence & Vertical Motion
- **Monday — Dynamics / Theory:** Develop relative/planetary/absolute vorticity, divergence and stretching/tilting concepts and link them to vertical motion.
- **Tuesday — Quantitative Methods:** Work systems of ODEs and finite-difference estimates of divergence and vorticity from gridded wind data.
- **Wednesday — Analysis Laboratory:** Compute qualitative vorticity/divergence tendencies from upper-air fields and connect them to ascent/descent.
- **Thursday — Remote Sensing / NWP:** Use model vorticity, divergence and vertical-motion products while documenting diagnostic limitations.
- **Friday — Operational Forecast Shift:** Issue a forecast discussion connecting upper forcing to sensible weather without treating one diagnostic as deterministic.
- **Required forms:** Synoptic Diagnostic Worksheet, NWP Diagnostic Worksheet
- **Competencies:** vorticity, divergence, vertical-motion

### Year II Week 5 · Program Week 41 — Quasigeostrophic Diagnosis & Forcing for Ascent
- **Monday — Dynamics / Theory:** Use QG height-tendency/omega concepts, differential vorticity advection and thermal advection to diagnose large-scale vertical motion.
- **Tuesday — Quantitative Methods:** Apply second-order ODE concepts, boundary conditions and qualitative elliptic-equation reasoning to atmospheric diagnosis.
- **Wednesday — Analysis Laboratory:** Construct a multi-field QG ascent/descent diagnosis from 500-hPa vorticity, thickness and temperature advection.
- **Thursday — Remote Sensing / NWP:** Compare QG diagnosis with model omega, precipitation and satellite evolution.
- **Friday — Operational Forecast Shift:** Prepare a precipitation/ceiling forecast tied to physically consistent forcing rather than model precipitation alone.
- **Required forms:** Synoptic Diagnostic Worksheet, NWP Diagnostic Worksheet, Professional Forecast Briefing
- **Competencies:** qg-diagnosis, forcing, precipitation-forecast

### Year II Week 6 · Program Week 42 — UNIT V CHECKPOINT — Dynamics & Diagnostic Reasoning
- **Monday — Dynamics / Theory:** Synthesize Weeks 1–5: scale analysis, balanced flow, thermal wind, vorticity/divergence and QG vertical-motion diagnosis.
- **Tuesday — Quantitative Methods:** Cumulative ODE/vector/differential-diagnostic assessment with atmospheric applications.
- **Wednesday — Analysis Laboratory:** Complete an unseen synoptic case with defensible force/vertical-motion diagnosis.
- **Thursday — Remote Sensing / NWP:** Cross-check model, radar/satellite and observations for consistency and identify contradictions.
- **Friday — Operational Forecast Shift:** Deliver a 7-minute technical forecast briefing and defend the dynamical reasoning. Unit checkpoint ≥80% required.
- **Required forms:** Synoptic Diagnostic Worksheet, Professional Forecast Briefing, Forecast Verification Scorecard
- **Competencies:** integration, diagnosis, oral-defense

### Year II Week 7 · Program Week 43 — Frontogenesis, Air-Mass Transformation & Frontal Circulations
- **Monday — Dynamics / Theory:** Study kinematic frontogenesis/frontolysis, deformation, convergence, tilting and ageostrophic frontal circulations.
- **Tuesday — Quantitative Methods:** Apply deformation tensors/vector gradients and ODE stability ideas to frontal strengthening/weakening.
- **Wednesday — Analysis Laboratory:** Analyze a surface/upper-air frontal zone and diagnose temperature/moisture advection and frontogenesis.
- **Thursday — Remote Sensing / NWP:** Use radar/satellite and model cross-sections to evaluate frontal slope, clouds and precipitation bands.
- **Friday — Operational Forecast Shift:** Forecast frontal timing, precipitation type/intensity and wind shifts with explicit timing uncertainty.
- **Required forms:** Synoptic Diagnostic Worksheet, Professional Doppler Radar Analysis, Multispectral Satellite Analysis
- **Competencies:** frontogenesis, frontal-circulation, timing

### Year II Week 8 · Program Week 44 — Extratropical Cyclogenesis & Life Cycles
- **Monday — Dynamics / Theory:** Connect baroclinic development, upper waves, surface pressure falls, occlusion and cyclone life-cycle frameworks.
- **Tuesday — Quantitative Methods:** Use coupled first-order systems and tendency reasoning to connect upper forcing with surface response.
- **Wednesday — Analysis Laboratory:** Track cyclone evolution across successive analyses and identify deepening, occlusion and deformation zones.
- **Thursday — Remote Sensing / NWP:** Compare deterministic/ensemble cyclone tracks and satellite signatures of development.
- **Friday — Operational Forecast Shift:** Produce a 48-hour cyclone forecast with wind, precipitation and uncertainty corridors.
- **Required forms:** Synoptic Diagnostic Worksheet, NWP Diagnostic Worksheet, Probabilistic / Ensemble Forecast Brief
- **Competencies:** cyclogenesis, baroclinic-development, ensemble-track

### Year II Week 9 · Program Week 45 — Jet Streaks, Ageostrophic Circulations & Rapid Development
- **Monday — Dynamics / Theory:** Diagnose straight/curved jet streaks, entrance/exit regions, ageostrophic flow and coupling with lower-level baroclinic zones.
- **Tuesday — Quantitative Methods:** Use vector projections and second-order differential relationships to quantify along-/cross-jet components.
- **Wednesday — Analysis Laboratory:** Analyze jet-level wind, divergence and surface development in a rapidly evolving case.
- **Thursday — Remote Sensing / NWP:** Compare water-vapor imagery, model tropopause/jet fields and observed cyclone deepening.
- **Friday — Operational Forecast Shift:** Brief high-impact wind and precipitation risks associated with jet/front coupling.
- **Required forms:** Upper-Air & Thermodynamic Analysis, Synoptic Diagnostic Worksheet, Professional Forecast Briefing
- **Competencies:** jet-dynamics, ageostrophic-flow, rapid-development

### Year II Week 10 · Program Week 46 — Potential Vorticity & Dynamic Tropopause Thinking
- **Monday — Dynamics / Theory:** Introduce Ertel potential vorticity, conservation/invertibility concepts and dynamic-tropopause interpretation.
- **Tuesday — Quantitative Methods:** Work conservation-law ODEs and transformations; relate stratification and vorticity to PV changes.
- **Wednesday — Analysis Laboratory:** Identify PV anomalies and tropopause folds in upper-air/model analyses and connect them to surface development.
- **Thursday — Remote Sensing / NWP:** Compare water-vapor imagery with PV/tropopause products and stratospheric intrusions.
- **Friday — Operational Forecast Shift:** Use PV thinking as one component of a multi-source forecast diagnosis and state limitations.
- **Required forms:** Synoptic Diagnostic Worksheet, Multispectral Satellite Analysis, NWP Diagnostic Worksheet
- **Competencies:** potential-vorticity, tropopause, multi-source-synthesis

### Year II Week 11 · Program Week 47 — Mesoscale Boundaries, Convergence & Terrain Effects
- **Monday — Dynamics / Theory:** Study drylines, sea-breeze fronts, outflow boundaries, terrain circulations and mesoscale convergence zones.
- **Tuesday — Quantitative Methods:** Apply coordinate transformations, slope/gradient calculations and time-scale analysis to mesoscale motion.
- **Wednesday — Analysis Laboratory:** Map mesoscale boundaries from surface observations and identify favored zones for ascent/convective initiation.
- **Thursday — Remote Sensing / NWP:** Use high-resolution model guidance, radar and satellite loops to track boundary evolution.
- **Friday — Operational Forecast Shift:** Issue a short-fuse mesoscale forecast emphasizing boundary timing and uncertainty.
- **Required forms:** Mesoscale Analysis Worksheet, Professional Doppler Radar Analysis, Professional Forecast Briefing
- **Competencies:** mesoscale-boundaries, terrain, short-range-forecast

### Year II Week 12 · Program Week 48 — UNIT VI CHECKPOINT — Synoptic & Mesoscale Weather Systems
- **Monday — Dynamics / Theory:** Synthesize Weeks 7–11: fronts, cyclogenesis, jets, PV and mesoscale boundaries.
- **Tuesday — Quantitative Methods:** Cumulative vector/ODE/diagnostic calculations from an unseen weather-system case.
- **Wednesday — Analysis Laboratory:** Complete a full surface-to-tropopause analysis and defend the expected evolution.
- **Thursday — Remote Sensing / NWP:** Reconcile radar, satellite, observations, deterministic models and ensembles.
- **Friday — Operational Forecast Shift:** Deliver an operational forecast shift briefing. Unit checkpoint ≥80% required.
- **Required forms:** Synoptic Diagnostic Worksheet, Mesoscale Analysis Worksheet, Operational Forecast Shift Log
- **Competencies:** synoptic-integration, mesoscale-integration, decision-support

### Year II Week 13 · Program Week 49 — Doppler Radar: Velocity, Sampling & Storm Motion
- **Monday — Dynamics / Theory:** Advance radial-velocity interpretation, aliasing, range folding, beam geometry, storm-relative concepts and sampling limitations.
- **Tuesday — Quantitative Methods:** Use vector projections and trigonometry to relate true motion/wind to radial velocity and storm motion.
- **Wednesday — Analysis Laboratory:** Analyze paired reflectivity/velocity data and distinguish convergence, divergence, rotation and artifacts.
- **Thursday — Remote Sensing / NWP:** Use NWS radar products and MetEd cases to document evidence versus interpretation.
- **Friday — Operational Forecast Shift:** Prepare a radar-based nowcast with explicit uncertainty and no unsupported warning claims.
- **Required forms:** Professional Doppler Radar Analysis, Professional Forecast Briefing
- **Competencies:** doppler-radar, sampling, nowcasting

### Year II Week 14 · Program Week 50 — Dual-Polarization Radar & Hydrometeor Classification
- **Monday — Dynamics / Theory:** Study ZDR, correlation coefficient, differential phase/KDP and conceptual hydrometeor classification.
- **Tuesday — Quantitative Methods:** Interpret logarithmic ratios, gradients and uncertainty; compare multi-variable radar signatures.
- **Wednesday — Analysis Laboratory:** Evaluate dual-pol signatures for heavy rain, hail, melting layer and nonmeteorological returns.
- **Thursday — Remote Sensing / NWP:** Cross-check radar interpretation with surface reports, satellite and environmental profiles.
- **Friday — Operational Forecast Shift:** Build a heavy-rain/hail decision-support briefing with evidence hierarchy and confidence.
- **Required forms:** Dual-Polarization Radar Analysis, Upper-Air & Thermodynamic Analysis, Impact-Based Decision Support Brief
- **Competencies:** dual-pol, hydrometeors, quality-control

### Year II Week 15 · Program Week 51 — Multispectral Satellite Meteorology
- **Monday — Dynamics / Theory:** Advance visible, infrared, water-vapor, near-IR and multispectral-composite interpretation for clouds, moisture and dynamics.
- **Tuesday — Quantitative Methods:** Relate radiance/brightness temperature, weighting functions and spatial/temporal sampling to interpretation.
- **Wednesday — Analysis Laboratory:** Analyze cloud-top evolution, moisture gradients, fog/low cloud and upper-level features in multispectral imagery.
- **Thursday — Remote Sensing / NWP:** Use NOAA GOES imagery and compare with radar/model fields to identify consistency and gaps.
- **Friday — Operational Forecast Shift:** Create a satellite-led nowcast and explain which conclusions are observations versus derived interpretation.
- **Required forms:** Multispectral Satellite Analysis, Professional Forecast Briefing
- **Competencies:** satellite, multispectral, observation-vs-inference

### Year II Week 16 · Program Week 52 — NWP Foundations: Discretization, Resolution & Parameterization
- **Monday — Dynamics / Theory:** Study primitive-equation modeling conceptually, grids, time stepping, truncation error, stability and parameterized processes.
- **Tuesday — Quantitative Methods:** Apply numerical Euler/Runge–Kutta ideas to ODEs; examine numerical stability and error growth.
- **Wednesday — Analysis Laboratory:** Compare model solutions at different resolutions and identify features that are resolved versus parameterized.
- **Thursday — Remote Sensing / NWP:** Inspect model fields for convection, boundary-layer, cloud and precipitation parameterization impacts.
- **Friday — Operational Forecast Shift:** Write a model-guidance memo that documents resolution limits and avoids treating output as ground truth.
- **Required forms:** NWP Diagnostic Worksheet, Model & Ensemble Comparison
- **Competencies:** nwp, numerical-methods, parameterization

### Year II Week 17 · Program Week 53 — Initialization, Data Assimilation & Ensemble Forecasting
- **Monday — Dynamics / Theory:** Introduce observation operators, background/analysis concepts, data assimilation, perturbations and ensemble design.
- **Tuesday — Quantitative Methods:** Use probability distributions, spread, rank concepts and ODE sensitivity to initial conditions.
- **Wednesday — Analysis Laboratory:** Compare analyses and ensemble members to observations; identify sensitive forecast elements.
- **Thursday — Remote Sensing / NWP:** Use operational ensemble guidance to assess track/timing/threshold probabilities.
- **Friday — Operational Forecast Shift:** Deliver a probabilistic forecast briefing with plausible alternatives and decision thresholds.
- **Required forms:** Probabilistic / Ensemble Forecast Brief, Model & Ensemble Comparison, Forecast Verification Scorecard
- **Competencies:** data-assimilation, ensembles, probability

- **Tuesday — Quantitative Methods:** Cumulative ODE, vector, diagnostic and numerical-method examination.
- **Wednesday — Analysis Laboratory:** Complete an unseen multi-scale case from observations through forecast reasoning.
- **Thursday — Remote Sensing / NWP:** Integrate radar, satellite, deterministic and ensemble guidance and document contradictions.
- **Friday — Operational Forecast Shift:** Complete a simulated forecast shift, written discussion and oral defense. Semester III requires ≥80% at every component.
