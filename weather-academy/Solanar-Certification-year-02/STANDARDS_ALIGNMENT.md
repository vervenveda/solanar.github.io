# Standards & University Alignment — Year II

## Purpose
Year II is designed as advanced post-secondary preparation for professional meteorology and operational forecasting. It is informed by, but does not itself confer, government employment eligibility, WMO recognition, university credit, or professional licensure.

## U.S. federal meteorologist (GS-1340) academic areas addressed across Years I–II
- Atmospheric dynamics and thermodynamics
- Synoptic/mesoscale analysis and prediction
- Physical meteorology
- Remote sensing / instrumentation
- Calculus-based physics
- Ordinary differential equations
- Supporting work in statistics, computer science, hydrology, climatology and related physical-science areas

The U.S. federal qualification standard also requires a qualifying degree or accepted combination of education/experience. Completing this Solanar certificate alone should not be represented as satisfying that degree requirement.

## WMO-informed professional competency orientation
The curriculum emphasizes: continual analysis/monitoring; forecasting; interpretation of NWP and observations; synthesis across sources; communication; quality/verification; specialized aviation, marine and tropical applications; and evidence of competency through assessed performance.

## University-program comparison
The course sequence intentionally overlaps common advanced atmospheric-science subjects found in current university programs: atmospheric dynamics, synoptic/mesoscale meteorology, radar, remote sensing, numerical modelling, weather forecasting, tropical meteorology, hydrometeorology and quantitative methods.
