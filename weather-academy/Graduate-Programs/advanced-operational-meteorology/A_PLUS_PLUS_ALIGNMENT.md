# A++ Graduate Design & External Benchmark Notes

This curriculum is intentionally broader than a single operational short course. It combines advanced academic atmospheric science, professional warning/forecast training concepts, quantitative mathematics, research methodology, reproducibility and defended performance.

## Benchmark families used in design
- Graduate atmospheric-science subjects such as dynamic meteorology, numerical weather prediction and data assimilation.
- NWS Warning Decision Training Division severe-weather training: mesoanalysis, convection-allowing models, data fusion, storm hazards, forecast challenges and warning decision making.
- NWS Radar & Applications Course themes: radar theory/operations, dual-polarization, MRMS, convective storm evolution, flash floods and human role in warnings.
- WMO competency orientation: analysis/monitoring, forecasting, NWP interpretation, multi-source synthesis and professional communication.
- Khaemenes Higher Learning mathematics pathways for Calculus II, Linear Algebra, Differential Equations, Statistics and advanced research mathematics.

## “Above standards” means
The program intentionally combines domains that institutions and agencies may teach separately. It does not claim accreditation, universal transfer credit, government qualification, or superiority to every individual university program. Those are institution-specific judgments.

## Graduate evidence
Completion requires:
- advanced derivations and quantitative work;
- repeated real/archived case analysis;
- radar/satellite/model/ensemble data fusion;
- six qualifying checkpoints;
- two semester comprehensives;
- displaced-real-time forecast/warning cases;
- research literature critique and replication;
- reproducible capstone research;
- five-part final qualifying review;
- oral defense.
