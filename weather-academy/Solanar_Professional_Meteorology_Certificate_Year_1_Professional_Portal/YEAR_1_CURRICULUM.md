# Solanar Professional Meteorology Certificate
## Year I · Common University Atmospheric Science Core

**Career destinations supported by the two-year program:** Operational Meteorologist · Broadcast Meteorologist · Climatologist

Year I is intentionally shared by all three pathways. Students should not specialize before establishing the mathematics, physics, atmospheric science, data-analysis, observation, and communication foundation expected in serious meteorology study.

## Program Design

- Two-year certificate
- 72 instructional weeks total
- Four 18-week semesters
- Year I: Semesters I–II (36 weeks)
- Year II: Semesters III–IV (career specialization)
- Year I recommended load: 32 semester-credit-equivalent hours
- Full-time workload target: approximately 35–40 total learning hours per week including class, problem sets, laboratory work, coding, reading, forecast practice and independent study
- Hard mastery threshold: 80%

## Important University-Credit Statement

This curriculum is designed as university-level preparation and as evidence that can support placement, challenge-exam, prior-learning, or transfer-credit evaluation. It cannot guarantee that a particular university will permit a student to sit a final examination, waive residency requirements, or award academic credit. Those decisions belong to the receiving institution.

## Daily Instructional Rhythm

Each instructional week follows a university-style five-day rhythm:

1. **Monday — Theory & Derivation:** atmospheric-science lecture, vocabulary, governing concepts and mathematics.
2. **Tuesday — Mathematics & Physics:** quantitative problem sets tied directly to meteorological applications.
3. **Wednesday — Laboratory & Instrumentation:** experiment, observation, sounding/map analysis or remote-sensing practical.
4. **Thursday — Computing, Data & Research:** scientific programming, statistical analysis, source evaluation and reproducible notebooks.
5. **Friday — Forecast / Communication Studio & Mastery:** technical forecast, public explanation or climate summary followed by mastery assessment.

Every instructional day includes reading/problem work plus a laboratory, data, computation, or communication artifact.

## Hard 80% Mastery Architecture

A student does not advance simply because an average is above 80%. Each required competency must independently reach the threshold.

- Daily exit assessment: **≥80%**
- Weekly mastery quiz/problem set: **≥80%**
- Laboratory practical/rubric: **≥80%**
- Mathematics problem-set mastery: **≥80%**
- Physics problem-set mastery: **≥80%**
- Coding/data practical: **≥80%**
- 6-week unit checkpoint: **≥80%**
- Semester midterm: **≥80% in every strand**
- Semester final: **≥80% in every strand**
- Year I comprehensive final/practical/oral defense: **≥80% in every required component**

Failed work is remediated and reassessed; it is not averaged away by stronger scores elsewhere.

## Year I Outcomes

By the end of Week 36, the student should be able to:

- use SI and meteorological units accurately;
- maintain a traceable observational/laboratory notebook;
- explain atmospheric composition and vertical structure;
- apply calculus concepts to rates, gradients, accumulation and atmospheric change;
- solve calculus-based introductory physics problems relevant to atmosphere and remote sensing;
- use gas-law, hydrostatic, moisture and adiabatic reasoning;
- diagnose basic atmospheric stability and cloud/precipitation processes;
- interpret station observations, surface maps and upper-air profiles;
- explain and interpret weather radar and satellite imagery at an introductory university level;
- describe numerical weather prediction, resolution, parameterization and ensemble uncertainty;
- calculate introductory forecast-verification and statistical measures;
- write reproducible scientific data analyses;
- separate observations, forecasts, model guidance, official warnings, research and news sources;
- deliver a technical forecast briefing and a public-facing weather explanation;
- distinguish short-term weather variability from long-term climate information;
- demonstrate enough common-core mastery to enter a Year II Operational, Broadcast, or Climatology specialization.

## Year II Preview

### Operational Meteorology Track
Synoptic and mesoscale dynamics · radar/dual-pol · satellite · numerical weather prediction · ensemble forecasting · severe convection · tropical meteorology · winter weather · hydrometeorology · aviation/marine meteorology · warning operations · forecast-office capstone.

### Broadcast Meteorology Track
Operational forecasting core · broadcast writing · weather graphics · studio performance · live severe-weather communication · digital/social weather · visual storytelling · pronunciation and geographic literacy · legal/ethical issues · emergency communication · demo-reel capstone.

### Climatology Track
Climate dynamics · advanced statistics · time series · GIS · remote sensing · reanalysis · paleoclimate · climate models · detection/attribution · extremes · impacts · adaptation · research methods · reproducible computational climate capstone.

## Credential Recommendation

After both years, issue the primary credential as **Solanar Professional Meteorology Certificate** with one specialization shown on the transcript and certificate:

- Operational Meteorology
- Broadcast Meteorology
- Climatology

A student completing more than one Year II specialization could earn additional concentration endorsements without repeating the common Year I core.
