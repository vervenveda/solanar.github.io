# Career Crosswalk

## Operational Meteorologist
Year I provides the mathematics, calculus-based physics, thermodynamics, observations, maps, soundings, radar, satellite, numerical-model, probability and forecast-verification foundation needed before advanced operational forecasting.

Year II should emphasize synoptic/mesoscale meteorology, numerical weather prediction, severe weather, tropical/winter weather, aviation/marine/hydrometeorology and warning operations.

## Broadcast Meteorologist
A broadcast meteorologist still needs authentic atmospheric-science competence. Year I therefore does not replace calculus, physics or thermodynamics with media classes.

Communication training begins in Year I so the student learns to translate evidence without distorting it. Year II should add studio presentation, graphics, live severe-weather coverage, digital media, audience psychology, geographic fluency and a professional demo reel while retaining substantial forecasting science.

## Climatologist
Year I establishes atmospheric physics, data handling, statistics, reproducibility, weather/climate distinction and model literacy.

Year II should deepen calculus/statistics, climate dynamics, time-series methods, GIS, remote sensing, reanalysis, paleoclimate, climate modeling, extremes, attribution, impacts and independent research.

## Common professional principle
All three tracks must be able to distinguish:
- observation,
- official alert/warning,
- forecast,
- numerical model guidance,
- derived analysis,
- research evidence,
- news/secondary reporting.

That source discipline is assessed throughout the program.
